# Portfolio design brief

> Working guidance for a more distinctive, evidence-led portfolio—not a validated way to distinguish human-made work from AI-generated work.

## Design direction

Differentiate the portfolio through art direction that is specific to the maker, work, audience, and context—not by applying a fashionable default template. Begin with what the portfolio can honestly show and what a visitor needs to understand; let that evidence determine the visual language.

### Visual rationale

- Build an editorial hierarchy that makes the maker's role, selected work, and project evidence easy to scan. Use carefully chosen type, scale, spacing, and layout to create a clear reading rhythm.
- Use purposeful asymmetry and compositional variety where they clarify relationships or give work room to speak. Do not default to a centered-everything page.
- Choose colors, type, and composition for a reason grounded in the work and its context. Favor restraint and consistency over decoration for decoration's sake.
- Use real, relevant project imagery or other assets when available and appropriate. Do not fabricate work, results, client marks, or personal details; do not substitute stock or generic decoration for meaningful evidence.

### Project evidence and honest case studies

Present projects as evidence, not just polished thumbnails or claims. Where information is available, make the problem, constraints, decisions, and the maker's specific contribution legible. State outcomes only when they can be verified; do not imply causality, metrics, ownership, or impact that the source material does not support. Keep case-study depth proportionate to the evidence actually available.

### Interaction, access, and responsive behavior

Use interaction and motion sparingly and purposefully: it should support orientation, feedback, or understanding rather than compete with the work. Respect reduced-motion preferences, including for motion triggered by interaction. Ensure content and controls remain usable across viewport sizes and with a keyboard; keep visible focus and semantic structure intact.

## Explicitly avoid

- A purple-to-blue gradient hero used as a generic signal of polish.
- Pervasive glassmorphism or blurred translucent panels without a contextual reason.
- Emoji or generic icon grids standing in for project evidence or meaningful navigation.
- A centered, generic hero as the default composition.
- Slogan-like generic copy that could describe any portfolio.
- Other template cues that obscure the maker's actual work, decisions, or voice.

These are not inherently forbidden techniques in every context; they are poor defaults when unmoored from the portfolio's content and purpose.

## Sources and rationale

- [Frontend Design skill: distinctive, context-led interface design](https://github.com/anthropics/skills/blob/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md?plain=1) — use a deliberate visual direction rather than generic defaults.
- [A List Apart: Art Direction and Design](https://alistapart.com/article/art-direction-and-design/) — art direction can connect a design's visual choices to its content and context.
- [Why Most AI-Generated Websites Still Feel Generic](https://dev.to/samareshdas/why-most-ai-generated-websites-still-feel-generic-and-what-actually-makes-a-product-feel-premium-33p2) — practical critique of generic, interchangeable presentation.
- [How to Break the AI-Generated UI Curse](https://dev.to/a_shokn/how-to-break-the-ai-generated-ui-curse-your-guide-to-authentic-professional-design-2en) — encourages intentional, context-specific interface decisions.
- [How to Structure a UX Case Study That Hiring Managers Notice](https://medium.com/@maxrichy/how-to-structure-a-ux-case-study-that-hiring-managers-notice-4f0e9bf45fa4) — supports communicating project context and decisions clearly.
- [How Do You Evaluate a Developer's Portfolio During Recruitment?](https://radleyjames.com/news-and-insights/industry-knowledge/how-do-you-evaluate-a-developers-portfolio-during-recruitment) — informs the emphasis on relevant, credible evidence.
- [WCAG 2.2: Animation from Interactions](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html) and [Technique C39](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Techniques/css/C39/) — inform reduced-motion support for interaction-triggered animation.

These references inform working design guidance; they do not establish a reliable human-versus-AI detector. The design should be judged by its fit, clarity, accessibility, and truthfulness—not by trying to infer its authorship from stylistic signals.
